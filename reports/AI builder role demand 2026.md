# Thin evidence points to two portfolio builds

**Bottom line: the evidence cannot tell you how large this market is, but it does show consistent signals about what employers ask for.** Every finding comes from search-result snippets. No posting was read in full, every attempt to fetch a job board failed in the research sandbox, and the samples are small: about 10 postings for Japan/Korea, about 12-15 for Germany/Netherlands/Poland, about 25 for France/Spain/Brazil, about 35 for global English roles, and 7 searches on portfolio expectations. Posting dates are mostly unverified, and many mid-2026 listings are probably closed as of 2026-11-03. Within that limit, three things recur. English-language "vibe coder" and AI-automation postings name **Claude Code and Cursor most often**, followed by n8n/Make/Zapier, with Lovable/Bolt/Replit and LLM/RAG/agent work behind them. Hiring-side advice, almost all from low-authority blogs, says a shipped, deployed, explainable project with evals beats many tutorial projects. And **truly worldwide, junior, employed roles are rare**: in non-English-speaking markets none was confirmed, and in the English sample perhaps 0-1. Most junior roles are locked to a country, to the EU, or to LATAM. I recommend two builds: a multilingual document-to-workflow automation product, and a tool-using multilingual support agent with a public eval harness. Both choices are inferences from thin data, designed to hit the most-repeated keywords and to give you proof that you can apply for roles with no stated experience requirement. The final section shows how to replace these inferences with measurements using the job-seek engine on your own machine.

## What the evidence base is, and what it cannot support

Treat this as a pilot scan, not a market study. The Japan/Korea, Germany/Netherlands/Poland and France/Spain/Brazil searches were done through a search tool whose summaries are model-written, and the research notes record that every WebFetch to remotive.com, jobgether.com, japan-dev.com, justjoin.it, jobgrid.eu, arc.dev and builtin.com failed because the domains were not reachable from the sandbox. Quotes are therefore limited to what the summaries reproduced, keyword tallies are rough ("Claude Code or Claude about 10, Cursor about 8, n8n/Make/Zapier about 7" across about 20 snippets, by the original researcher's own admission), and eligibility fields such as "Worldwide" or "Remote, JP" were not confirmed on primary pages. Aggregators also repeat the same posting, so the true count of independent employers is lower than the count of listings. Several figures in the notes come from vendor or SEO blogs, such as a claim of 1,842 active vibe-coding listings with 73% remote ([Sorceress](https://sorceress.games/blog/where-vibe-coding-jobs-are-pay-skills-portfolio-in-2026)) and typical pay of $120-190k ([Base44](https://base44.com/guides/vibe-coding-jobs)). I do not use either as evidence. Nothing here is a measurement of demand volume.

## Non-English-speaking markets: almost nothing is both remote and open to India

The priority markets gave the weakest results, and the weakness is itself the main finding.

**Japan and Korea.** The clearest overseas-friendly role found was Money Forward's Staff AI Engineer, which lists overseas visa sponsorship and "English: Business Level is enough", but it is senior and involves relocation, not remote work ([japan-dev](https://japan-dev.com/jobs/money-forward/money-forward-staff-ai-engineer--cdao-office-tokyojapan-dev-0g5gi4)). The only junior-looking worldwide-remote item was カサナレ's AI Backend Engineer at level L1-L2, listed at 4,080,000-6,880,000 JPY, a backend role rather than LLM-specific, with language requirements not seen ([Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/artificial-intelligence/ai-backend-engineer-l1-l2-4745517)). Other fits fail on one criterion each: a fully remote CareerCross AI Engineer role needs more than 3 years ([CareerCross](https://www.careercross.com/en/job/detail-1585612?page=11)), an "Agent Harness Engineer" role is hybrid ([CareerCross](https://www.careercross.com/en/job/pdf-1605836)), and Moro Tech's "Remote, JP" generative-AI role may be Japan-residents-only ([Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/software-development/software-engineer-generative-ai-engineering-4608077)). In Korea, no remote, English-only, abroad-eligible role turned up. Cohere's Korea role needs Korean and in-country presence ([Inference Jobs](https://www.inferencejobs.com/companies/cohere/jobs/applied-ai-engineer-agentic-workflows-korea)), and Sendbird's roles are Seoul-hybrid and senior ([Sendbird](https://talent.emcap.com/companies/sendbird/jobs/66613619-software-engineer-ai-agent)). The pattern is that Japanese employers open to people abroad mostly use "Apply from Abroad" (relocation plus visa) rather than true remote, and the engine's own README notes that Japan's work visa generally needs a degree or about 10 years' experience. A Remote Rocketship page reportedly listed 15 junior Japan AI Engineer roles at 1-2 years, but no individual posting was seen ([Remote Rocketship](https://www.remoterocketship.com/country/japan/jobs/ai-engineer)), so I count it only as a lead to check.

**Germany, Netherlands, Poland.** Of roughly 12 postings, **none was confirmed worldwide-eligible**. The junior roles are residence-locked: Monterail's Junior AI Developer is Poland-only at about 31.50 PLN/h ([Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/artificial-intelligence/junior-ai-developer-4871142)); Blazity's Junior AI Engineer (Next.js & LLM) is "100% remote from Poland" ([justjoin.it](https://justjoin.it/offers/blazity-sp-z-o-o--junior-ai-engineer-next-js-llm--warszawa-javascript)); a German AI Engineer role is "Nur DE" ([remotely.de](https://www.remotely.de/job/asociacion-generacion-21-ai-enginer)). Dutch and Europe-remote roles that name Claude Code and Cursor skew senior: Trengo's Senior AI Engineer uses Cursor and Claude Code daily ([jobsglitch](https://jobsglitch.com/jobs/senior-ai-engineer-at-trengo-utrecht-office-6a19d6ca58b93c861e66e443)), and an onhires AI Product Engineer role in Europe-remote mentions agent tools such as Claude Code and Codex, which is a paraphrase from a summary and not a verified quote ([jobgrid](https://jobgrid.eu/fr/jobs/ai-product-engineer-onhires/)). The one lead that might admit a non-Polish contractor is Monterail's freelance LLM Engineer network, which is B2B-only at 90-140 PLN/h and focused on "practical engineering solutions over traditional ML research" ([remoteleaf](https://remoteleaf.com/company/monterail/ai-engineer-freelancer-poland/)). Whether it accepts applicants from India is unverified.

**France, Spain, Brazil.** Of about 25 listings, almost all were locked to Brazil, LATAM, Spain, the EU/EMEA or a country of residence. The only "worldwide" claims were TechMagic's AI Engineer role (via an aggregator, anchored to Sao Paulo) ([jobera](https://jobera.com/job/techmagic-ai-engineer-remote-in-brazil-240887/)) and a Jobgether "Product Engineer, AI" listing described as "work from anywhere" with unclear country scope ([Lever/Jobgether](https://jobs.lever.co/jobgether/fb8f2276-852e-4708-bc34-8306e72d83c7)); neither was verified. Explicitly junior roles (BrandBastion Barcelona, Lighthouse Spain, a Cadiz posting) are country-locked ([echojobs](https://echojobs.io/job/brandbastion-junior-ai-engineer-gywul), [Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/artificial-intelligence/junior-data-science-engineer-4639747)). Brazil is where the automation niche is densest: Pyyne's AI Automation Engineer names n8n, Zapier, Make, LangChain and OpenAI/Claude/Gemini ([echojobs](https://echojobs.io/job/pyyne-ai-automation-engineer-2t8bp)), and Aloware's contractor role names n8n, scripts, APIs and CRM ([glich](https://hw.glich.co/jobboard/jobs/ai-automation-engineer-at-aloware-x463i7)), but both require Brazil or neighbouring residence, and Aloware's posting is dated December 2025. LATAM reposts demand strong English, such as "Excellent English — written and spoken" for Trtl ([opentoworkremote](https://www.opentoworkremote.com/view/1454251)), and pay USD 3,000-4,500 a month for a Forward Deployed AI Engineer ([opentoworkremote](https://www.opentoworkremote.com/view/1464325)); these are mostly senior and LATAM-resident. The "vibe coder" title was not found at any French, Spanish or Brazilian employer. The one near match, an "AI-native Software Developer" in Ile-de-France, is regional ([JobTarget](https://www.jobtarget.com/jobs/jt-vaoaaojrq6/ai-native-software-developer-chatenay-en-france-ile-de-france)).

My reading: for a candidate in India, the non-English-speaking-country route is mostly **relocation plus visa**, or contractor arrangements that these snippets cannot confirm, and not open remote employment. That is a hypothesis consistent with the sample, not a proven fact.

The English sample (about 35 postings or posting families) is the richest and still thin. The tool signal is clear. A Remotive "Claude Code expert" vibe-coder role asks for apps shipped with Claude Code and a deployed-project portfolio ([Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/ai-ml/vibe-coder-ai-developer-claude-code-expert-4281964)). Creative Fabrica's vibe-coder role asks for micro-products built with Lovable, Claude and Cursor, success measured by usage ([Greenhouse](https://job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/creativefabrica/jobs/4815567101)). An Arc part-time contract makes Bolt, Lovable, Replit and Cursor mandatory but asks 3-5 years ([Arc](https://arc.dev/remote-jobs/details/ai-native-vibe-coder-bolt-lovable-replit-cursor-part-time-emea-apac-mvry8plmzh)). A Europe-remote AI Product Engineer posting asks candidates to "ship MVPs, experiments, and production components quickly" with Claude Code and Codex ([Weekday](https://jobs.weekday.works/ai-product-engineer-agentic-ai-vibe-coding-at-kuubiik-4383059446)). Bamboo Works reposted a Claude Code Developer family across at least six listings on several boards, emphasising hands-on Claude Code CLI, strong written English and "translating non-technical operational requests into technical requirements" ([Arc](https://arc.dev/remote-jobs/j/bamboo-works-claude-developer-ox9z9nypgj), [Working Nomads](https://www.workingnomads.com/jobs/claude-code-developer-bamboo-works)). Automation roles add n8n/Make/Zapier, APIs, webhooks and JSON: Later's AI Automation Engineer Co-op asks candidates to interview non-technical staff and "wire together LLMs, agents, and prompt design patterns" ([Greenhouse](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/later/jobs/8604889002)); an Ayatix Labs role in Bengaluru wants production Make.com with routers and error handlers plus Python at about 2 years ([Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/jobs/4325821-ai-workflow-automation-engineer)).

Junior examples include a Built In "AI Software Developer (Junior) – AI-First / Vibecoding" with Replit, Cursor and Claude Code as nice-to-haves ([Built In](https://builtin.com/job/ai-software-developer-junior-ai-first-vibecoding/9338268)), a Zazz.io entry-level AI-Assisted Developer / Prompt Engineer ([Jobgether](https://jobgether.com/offer/69ec355ec646310ee39aec0f-ai-assisted-developer-prompt-engineer-entry-level)), and a Dice Junior Vibe Engineer needing Python, SQL, AWS basics and Git ([Dice](https://www.dice.com/job-detail/f0f3d754-977b-49cf-a42e-69ede5151cdf)). The worldwide candidates are a part-time Jobhire vibe coder on Remotive ("Location: Worldwide", about a month old and possibly closed) ([Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/software-development/vibe-coder-4240512)) and Arc's part-time "Expert Vibe Coders WW PT" ([Arc](https://arc.dev/remote-jobs/details/expert-vibe-coders-ww-pt-ozv5wmpyss)). Pay in worldwide-style postings is low and sparse: $600-1,500 a month on OnlineJobs.ph ([OnlineJobs.ph](https://www.onlinejobs.ph/jobseekers/job/Vibe-coding-AI-SaaS-Developer-ClaudeCursor-Expert-600-1500-USDMonth-Vibe-Coding-Workflow-1530726)) and $30-35 an hour for part-time "AI pilot" vibe-coding gigs ([Jobicy](https://jobicy.com/jobs/137190-ai-pilot-vibe-coding-assistant-freelance)). "No experience required" AI Generalist listings are mostly contractor AI-training work and often US-only ([Alignlist](https://alignlist.com/jobs/meridial-ai-generalist-no-experience-required-us-only-freelance-ai-trainer-project-4677212101)). Market context is discouraging for juniors but unverified: one survey claims 75% of founders now prioritise experienced hires ([CFOtech](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/ai-drives-hiring-shift-to-senior-devs-at-eu-startups)).

## What hiring-side voices say about portfolios, and how much to trust it

The portfolio evidence is opinion, not data. It comes from career blogs, prep sites and Reddit mirrors, and no first-party named hiring-manager quote was obtained. The consistent message is that three or four deeply documented, deployed projects beat a dozen tutorials, and that a README with an evaluation suite and results table is called "the #1 portfolio signal" by one unverified source ([dataexpert](https://www.dataexpert.io/blog/ultimate-guide-ai-engineering-portfolios), [dev.to](https://dev.to/rishi_kora/show-your-work-the-ai-engineer-proof-of-work-portfolio-2f2k)). AI-native companies reportedly want shipped work visible before the first conversation, and the strongest signal is having watched customers use the product ([provn.co](https://provn.co/blog/2026/06/get-hired-early-career-builder)). The recurring filter is explainability: Reddit commenters say hiring managers probe debugging without AI and reasoning about decisions, and that stating what AI did versus what you changed is more credible than hiding it ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qe960j/snapshots/2026-01-16T20%3A50%3A45.135165Z)). Generic chatbots and basic RAG demos are called undifferentiating, with recommended minimums of a RAG app, a tool-using agent and an authenticated full-stack product ([datatalks.club](https://datatalks.club/podwiki/wiki/rag-portfolio-projects/), [Coderhouse](https://www.coderhouse.com/articles/ai-engineer-portfolio-projects-llm-apps)). Take-homes mirror this: Replit's published process includes a take-home or project walkthrough ([Replit](https://repl.it/jobs)), and third-party guides describe a 4-8 hour deployed build judged on product judgment and a tradeoff writeup ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233476795/what-the-replit-interview-actually-tests/)). A skeptical view exists: side projects are noise unless many people use them ([Reddit mirror](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1ps4yet/snapshots/2025-12-26T18%3A31%3A31.46825Z)). Claims about real-user counts, cost/latency metrics, observability, MCP servers and open-source traction as hiring signals were **not** supported by any source found, so I treat them as my judgment, not evidence.

## Demand gaps: what the evidence supports and what it does not

Three gaps are reasonably supported. First, an **eligibility gap**: junior roles exist but are region-locked, while worldwide roles are part-time, low-paid or senior, so a candidate in India competes mainly for contractor, part-time and relocation routes. Second, an **experience-proof gap**: postings that ask 0-2 years substitute "link to something shipped" and portfolio requirements for tenure, so evidence of shipping is the currency. Third, a **language-and-translation gap** that I infer: employers in non-English-speaking countries write postings in English for roles tagged "English: Business Level" or "Japanese not required", yet no posting in the sample asked for multilingual document handling, which is a place where an India-based builder can differentiate. That last point is my inference and not an observed demand. Not evidenced at all: MCP, voice AI, observability, and any quantified "under-supplied" project category. The notes found saturation claims for chatbots and basic RAG but no demand-gap data. A last caution: the "AI automation" niche has the lowest entry bar in the sample (n8n, Make, Zapier and APIs), but it is also where I found the most residence-locked roles (Brazil), so its low bar may mean heavy competition.

## Two projects to build

Both projects are designed so the repository, the live URL and the README together answer the questions the sources say hiring managers ask: what did you build, who used it, how do you know it works, and what did you decide versus the AI. All numbers on resumes must be your measured results. The bullets below use placeholders, never invent them.

### Project 1: LedgerFlow, a multilingual document-to-workflow automation platform

**Problem.** Small businesses in markets like Japan, Germany, France, Spain and Brazil receive invoices, order emails, and support requests in their own languages, and staff retype them into spreadsheets or a CRM. Existing no-code automations break on messy input and offer no accuracy numbers.

**Users.** An operations lead at a 5-50 person business who is non-technical (Later's posting emphasises interviewing non-technical staff; Bamboo Works emphasises translating non-technical requests), plus a reviewer who approves low-confidence extractions.

**Features.** (1) Intake from email forwarding, file upload and a webhook; (2) language detection and extraction of structured JSON (vendor, date, line items, totals, currency) from PDFs and emails in at least English, Japanese, German, French, Spanish and Portuguese; (3) a confidence score per field with a human-review queue for low confidence; (4) routing rules that push approved records to Google Sheets and a CRM through webhooks, with retries, idempotency keys and a dead-letter queue (the error-handler pattern in the Ayatix posting); (5) a visual workflow built in n8n for the integration layer, plus custom code for extraction and validation, so you can speak to both no-code and code; (6) an audit log showing what the model proposed and what the human changed; (7) a per-document cost and latency record.

**Architecture.** Next.js (TypeScript) front end and API routes; Postgres for jobs, extractions, reviews and audit logs; a queue (Postgres-based or a managed queue) for processing; Claude API with structured output and a JSON-schema validator; n8n self-hosted for integrations; deployment on Vercel plus a small container host for n8n. Development with Claude Code, with a committed `CLAUDE.md` and a short `DECISIONS.md` recording what AI wrote and what you changed.

**Stack.** TypeScript, Next.js, Postgres, Python for the eval scripts, n8n, Zod, Docker, Claude API.

**Keyword and requirement coverage (with evidence).**

| Requirement or keyword | Where it appears in the evidence | Covered by |
|---|---|---|
| n8n / Make / Zapier | Pyyne, Aloware, Later, Vilambo, Djinni junior n8n role | Integration layer in n8n, plus a short writeup of a Make equivalent |
| APIs, webhooks, JSON | Seamless/Ayatix/Nexa-type automation roles in global English notes | Webhook intake, JSON schema, routing |
| LLM and agent wiring, prompt design | Later co-op, Monterail, Blazity | Extraction prompts and versioned prompt set |
| Error handlers, routers | Ayatix (Make.com) | Retries, dead-letter queue, routing rules |
| CRM integration | Aloware, Nexa (GoHighLevel) | CRM push with idempotency |
| Translating non-technical requests | Bamboo Works, Later | A written discovery interview with 2-3 real small-business owners and a requirements doc in the repo |
| Claude Code workflow | Roughly the most-named tool across the English snippets | Build log, `CLAUDE.md`, commit history |
| Shipped, deployed, portfolio link | Vibe-coder postings, Creative Fabrica, hiring blogs | Live URL, demo video, README |

**Two-week plan.** Days 1-2: interview two or three real small-business owners (family or local contacts are fine), collect 40-60 real or realistically synthetic documents across six languages with no private data, and write the requirements doc and the labelled gold set. Days 3-4: schema, Postgres, Next.js shell, upload and webhook intake. Days 5-6: extraction pipeline with confidence scoring and validation. Days 7-8: review queue and audit log. Days 9-10: n8n workflows, CRM and Sheets push, retries and dead-letter handling. Days 11-12: eval harness run, prompt iteration, cost and latency logging. Day 13: deploy, onboard at least one real user, record the demo. Day 14: README, architecture diagram, `DECISIONS.md`, post a build-in-public writeup.

**Eval and metrics to report.** Field-level accuracy and exact-match on the gold set, broken down by language; document-level "needs human review" rate against actual error rate (calibration of confidence); end-to-end success rate through the integration layer including retried failures; median and p95 latency and cost per document; and the number of real documents processed by real users. Report failures honestly, including the language where accuracy was lowest.

**Demo script (about 90 seconds).** Forward a Japanese invoice and a German order email; show extraction with field confidences; open the review queue and correct one field; approve; show the record landing in the Sheet and CRM via n8n; trigger a deliberate downstream failure and show the retry and dead-letter handling; end on the accuracy-by-language table and cost per document.

**Resume bullets (fill with your measured numbers).** "Built and deployed a multilingual document-to-CRM automation (Next.js, Postgres, n8n, Claude API) processing [N] documents across [6] languages with [X]% field-level accuracy on a [N]-document labelled set." "Designed a confidence-based human review queue that cut manual review to [Y]% of documents at [Z]% post-review error." "Implemented retries, idempotency and a dead-letter queue; [N]% of injected downstream failures recovered automatically." "Built with Claude Code; documented AI-written versus hand-changed code in a decision log."

### Project 2: Polyglot Support Agent with an open eval harness

**Problem.** Companies hiring AI builders want agents that work reliably, and generic chatbots or basic RAG demos are called undifferentiating by the portfolio sources. A support agent that must answer in the customer's language, call tools, and prove its quality is a harder, more credible exhibit.

**Users.** A support lead at a small SaaS or e-commerce business who wants an agent that answers questions from their docs in the customer's language, looks up orders, and escalates; and the engineer who maintains it and needs regression tests.

**Features.** (1) Multilingual knowledge-base ingestion (Markdown and PDF docs) with retrieval and citations; (2) a tool-using agent with at least three tools (order lookup, refund eligibility check, ticket creation against a mock backend) and explicit guardrails (refunds above a threshold escalate to a human); (3) language matching, with the answer in the user's language and the citation shown in the source language; (4) authenticated admin UI with conversation traces, tool calls and per-turn cost; (5) the **eval harness as a first-class feature**: a versioned test set of at least 100 cases in at least five languages, deterministic checks (tool called, correct arguments, citation present, no forbidden action) plus an LLM judge calibrated against your own hand labels; (6) a CI job that runs the evals on each prompt or model change and fails on regression; (7) optional, since MCP is not evidenced in the sample: expose the order and ticket tools as an MCP server, framed as an extra and not a core requirement.

**Architecture.** Next.js (TypeScript) UI and API; Python service for ingestion and the eval runner; Postgres with pgvector for chunks and traces; Claude API with tool use; GitHub Actions for evals on pull requests; auth via a managed provider; deployed publicly with a read-only demo tenant.

**Stack.** TypeScript, Next.js, Python, Postgres with pgvector, Claude API, GitHub Actions.

**Keyword and requirement coverage (with evidence).**

| Requirement or keyword | Where it appears in the evidence | Covered by |
|---|---|---|
| LLM, RAG, agents, memory, rapid MVP | OnlineJobs.ph AI Agent Developer, Snowplow, Rozeta Labs, Puente | Retrieval, tool-using agent, session memory |
| Next.js plus LLM | Blazity junior role | Full-stack app |
| Production LLM applications, agentic systems | LATAM senior reposts | Traces, guardrails, cost tracking |
| Evaluation | Bjak LLM Application Engineer (term seen in a snippet); portfolio sources call evals the top signal | Eval harness and CI regression gate |
| Python, SQL, Git, cloud basics | Dice junior, BrandBastion | Python eval runner, Postgres, deployment |
| Claude Code or Cursor in daily work | Trengo, Kuubiik, Bamboo Works | Build log and commit history |
| Product sense and measured use | Cursor's "every engineer needs product sense", Creative Fabrica usage metric | Interview with a real support owner; usage and resolution metrics from a pilot |

**Two-week plan.** Days 1-2: pick a real or realistic support domain, write 100+ test cases across the languages with expected tool calls, and set the success criteria before building. Days 3-5: ingestion, retrieval with citations, baseline agent. Days 6-7: tools and guardrails. Days 8-9: eval runner, deterministic checks, judge, and calibration against your hand labels. Days 10-11: iterate prompts and retrieval against the harness and record every change with its effect. Day 12: CI regression gate and trace UI. Day 13: deploy, pilot with a real small shop or community, record the demo. Day 14: README with the results table, tradeoffs writeup, and a post.

**Eval and metrics to report.** Task success rate on the test set, overall and per language; tool-call correctness; citation accuracy; hallucination or unsupported-claim rate under your judge, with judge-versus-human agreement stated; escalation precision and recall; median and p95 latency and cost per conversation; before-and-after table for each prompt or retrieval change; and real pilot conversations handled. Publish the failures.

**Demo script (about 2 minutes).** Ask the same refund question in Spanish and Japanese; show retrieval citations and the tool call; show a high-value refund escalating; open the trace and cost; then make a deliberately bad prompt change, push, and show CI failing on the regression table; close on the per-language results.

**Resume bullets (fill with your measured numbers).** "Built a tool-using multilingual support agent (Next.js, Python, pgvector, Claude API) with a [N]-case, [5]-language eval harness; task success rose from [A]% to [B]% over [K] measured iterations." "Added a CI regression gate that blocks prompt changes lowering success by more than [X] points." "Calibrated an LLM judge to [Y]% agreement with hand labels." "Ran a pilot with [N] real conversations at [$] median cost and [ms] p95 latency."

**Why these two and not others.** Together they cover the two clusters the notes support: no-code and integration automation (n8n, APIs, CRM) and LLM application engineering with agents and evals. Both have a visible Claude Code build trail, both are deployed, both ship published numbers, and both put multilingual handling at the centre, which matches the non-English-speaking-employer priority without requiring you to know those languages deeply. The weakness is that I picked them from keyword frequency in snippets. The existing `PROJECTS.md` in the repo already proposes a shipped SaaS, an agent with evals, and an MCP tool, and these two align with its items 2 and 3, so Project 2 is a more specific version of its agent-with-evals idea.

## Verify and extend this evidence with the job-seek engine

The engine in `/home/user/js` is built to replace this snippet-based pilot with a measured sample, but run it on your own machine because the research sandbox could not reach most job sites. Its README says its sources are unverified from the build sandbox, so test each URL locally first.

**Setup.** Run `npm i && npx playwright install chromium`, copy `profile/profile.example.yml` to `profile/profile.yml` and fill it in truthfully, and export `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. Set the `international` block in the profile so non-English postings are translated, local-language requirements are detected, and roles requiring the local language are dropped. In that mode, overseas jobs are kept only if remote and open to India-based workers (tagged REMOTE-IN) or if they offer both visa sponsorship and relocation support. Set `filters` (`max_years`, `max_age_days`, `remote_only`) to match your target; stale posts then drop out, which addresses the recency problem in this report.

**Collect.** Run `npm run scan && npm run rank`. Scan reads Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby public APIs, HN Who's Hiring and RemoteOK; add more through `sources.json` or the hub's Sources page. For small companies in Japan, Germany and elsewhere, use `npm run discover -- <directory-url-or-text-file>` with startup directories, accelerator portfolios, and the remoteintech/remote-jobs and awesome-remote-job lists the README suggests, then rescan so career pages are probed and new postings translated and filtered. This is the coverage this report lacked: the notes did not reach Wellfound, HN Who's Hiring, startup.jobs, Welcome to the Jungle or company career pages.

**Extract.** `package.json` defines no `extract` script even though the README mentions `npm run extract`; the command exists in `src/cli.ts`, so run `npx tsx src/cli.ts extract`, or use "Deep-analyse listings" in the Insights page. It has Claude pull must-haves, nice-to-haves, tech, AI concepts, responsibilities, traits and seniority from every ranked job scoring 40 or above. This reads the full listing text, which fixes this report's core limitation, and it will cost API usage, so start with a small batch.

**Read Insights.** Start the hub with `npm run web:install && npm run web` and open http://localhost:3000. Insights shows each keyword with its count, percentage of listings, real quotes and the listings behind it, and it filters by scope (all, 75+, remote, low competition) and group.
